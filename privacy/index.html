Privacy policy

Fino for iPhone · Effective 3 October 2026

Fino has no account, no analytics and no ads. What you record stays on your iPhone, with a backup in your own iCloud Drive while backups are on. Unless you choose to share with other Fino users (below), nothing leaves it, except the name of a coffee you add, which Fino looks up in its roaster’s shop.

What Fino stores, and where

Your setups, coffees, brews, tasting notes and the photos you take of bags are saved in Fino’s storage on your iPhone. They’re included in your iPhone’s backups (iCloud Backup or your computer), like any app’s data, and Fino backs them up to your iCloud Drive (below). Fino doesn’t send them anywhere else.

iCloud Drive backups

So you don’t lose your coffee if you lose your iPhone or delete the app, Fino saves a copy of your setups, coffees, brews, notes and bag photos to Fino’s folder in your own iCloud Drive. It does this whenever something changes, and keeps your most recent copies. They’re stored by Apple in your iCloud account, under Apple’s privacy policy and your iCloud storage, and only you can reach them. The developer can’t see or read them. Backups are on when you’re signed in to iCloud with iCloud Drive turned on, and you can turn them off in Fino under You › iCloud backup. When you reinstall Fino or set up a new iPhone, it offers to restore the most recent copy.

The camera and your photos

When you photograph a bag’s label or its beans, Fino reads the picture on your iPhone, with Apple’s text recognition and, where available, Apple Intelligence’s on-device model. Photos you choose from your library are read the same way. The photo itself is never uploaded.

Looking up a coffee you add

After reading a label, or when you type a coffee’s name, Fino looks for the coffee’s page in its roaster’s shop, to add the shop’s photo and what the label or you leave out. It sends only what names the coffee: its name to the roaster’s own shop, through the shop’s search, when Fino knows the shop’s site (printed on the bag, or from your other coffees from that roaster); otherwise to DuckDuckGo, as a web search for the roaster and the coffee’s name. DuckDuckGo and the shop see an ordinary web request from your iPhone (your IP address, for example), under their own privacy policies. A page is used only when it’s the same coffee; what the bag says always stays. To send nothing, turn off “Look them up online” in You › Privacy.

Shop links

When you paste or share a link to a coffee’s page in a shop, Fino downloads that page and its product photo directly from the shop, as a browser would. To redraw a coffee’s picture after an update, it may download them again once, the same way. The shop sees an ordinary web request from your iPhone (your IP address, for example), under its own privacy policy. “Buy again” and the web search for a coffee open in your browser, and only when you tap them.

Roaster logos

To show a roaster’s logo beside its coffees, Fino downloads it from the roaster’s own website: the site in a shop link you added for one of that roaster’s coffees, or a roaster’s site that comes with the app. It fetches the site’s home page and its icon directly, as a browser would, and keeps the logo on your iPhone, so it asks again only if no logo was found, a week later at the earliest. The roaster’s site sees an ordinary web request from your iPhone (your IP address, for example), under its own privacy policy. Nothing about you or your coffees is sent.

Apple Intelligence

On iPhones that support it, Fino uses Apple’s on-device language model to read unusual wording on labels and in your brew notes. It runs on your iPhone. The numbers Fino shows always come from what you wrote or from its own rules.

Reminders, widgets and Siri

Reminders (when a coffee has rested, when a cold brew is ready) are scheduled on your iPhone. Widgets, the Lock Screen timer and Siri read a small summary Fino keeps on your iPhone. When you share a shop page to Fino, it waits in Fino’s storage until you open the app, for 14 days at most.

Sharing a recipe

A recipe you share travels inside the link you send, to whoever you choose. Fino doesn’t upload it anywhere.

Sharing with other Fino users

In Fino under You › Privacy you choose how much you share. It starts at “Just me”, where nothing is sent and nothing is read from others.

  • Learn from others reads liked brews other people sent, for the brew methods you use, and turns them into typical settings on your iPhone. Fino sends nothing; your iPhone downloads from Apple’s servers, which see an ordinary request (your IP address, for example).
  • Help anonymously also sends, for each coffee you marked as liked, its roaster and name, roast level, brew method, grinder model and whether its zero is known, grind setting, dose, yield, time, water temperature, whether the basket was pressurized, the flow even and the machine warm, and the day (not the time), plus how much two shots’ times differed when only the grind changed. Never notes, photos, places, your name or your setup’s other details. Each person counts once, by the identifier Apple attaches to their records (below).
  • Share publicly also lets you pick a public name and publish recipes you choose, with the same details a shared link carries. Anyone with Fino can see them with your public name.

These are stored in the public database of Fino’s iCloud container, run by Apple under its privacy policy. You need to be signed in to iCloud to send anything. Apple attaches to each record an identifier that is unique to Fino and doesn’t reveal your Apple Account; the developer can see the records and that identifier, but not who you are. The identifier is the same on everything you send and other Fino users can read it, so if you publish under a public name, the brews you sent anonymously can be linked to that name. Reports you file on a published recipe are read only by Fino’s moderators, who can remove a recipe or ban an account; every copy of Fino then ignores what that account sends. Stepping back to a lower option deletes what the higher one sent: your contributions, your published recipes and your public name.

Crash reports and beta feedback

If you’ve chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone’s settings, Apple may send the developer anonymous crash reports. If you test Fino with TestFlight, the feedback and screenshots you choose to send reach the developer through Apple. Apple handles both under its own privacy policy.

What Fino doesn’t do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No analytics, advertising or tracking code, and no third-party SDKs.
  • No tracking across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared unless you turn on sharing.

Deleting your data

Delete a coffee or a brew in the app, or delete Fino to remove everything it stored on your iPhone. Deleting the app leaves Fino’s backups in your iCloud Drive, so you can restore them later. To remove them, open Settings › [your name] › iCloud on your iPhone and delete Fino’s data from your iCloud storage. Copies in your iPhone’s own backups follow the rules of iCloud or your computer. To delete what you shared with other Fino users, go back to “Just me” in You › Privacy before you delete the app; afterwards, write to the address below.

Children

Fino isn’t aimed at children.
